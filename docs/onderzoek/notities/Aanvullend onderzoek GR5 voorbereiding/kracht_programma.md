# Program type "Kracht" (strength and muscle first, endurance second) and the lighter "Fit en gezond" variant: weekly structure and progression

Scope: ASCEND schedules sessions onto days. MacroFactor Workouts (Smart Progression) handles exercise content (sets/reps/load). These notes therefore cover weekly structure: number of strength sessions, split, frequency per muscle, weekly set targets as *design constraints for templates*, cardio dose/modality/timing, deloads, 4-week phase progression, and progress/fatigue signals.

Evidence grading used throughout:
- **Strong**: multiple meta-analyses / large overview of reviews consistent
- **Moderate**: single meta-analysis or meta-regression, or consistent RCTs with limitations
- **Weak**: single small RCT, preliminary/subgroup findings
- **Expert opinion**: Delphi consensus, coaching sources, my inference

Note on sourcing: Several primary pages (PubMed, Springer) could not be fetched directly (cookie/redirect walls); some numbers below come from search-result abstracts and secondary summaries. These are flagged where relevant.

## 1. Training frequency per muscle group and weekly sets (hypertrophy vs strength); does split matter when volume is equal?

### Takeaway
Weekly volume, not frequency or split, is the main driver of hypertrophy. Gains rise with more sets but with diminishing returns, and the first ~10 weekly sets per muscle bring the largest return. Frequency matters little for size once volume is equal, but it does add to strength gains (practice effect). Practical target for a recreational "Kracht" trainee: each muscle trained 2x/week, about 10 (range 8–15) hard sets per muscle per week. Split choice is a scheduling/preference decision. (Strong evidence for "volume > split"; moderate for exact set numbers.)

### Cited Findings
- **Frequency, volume-equated (hypertrophy)**: Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019 (J Sports Sci 37:1286–1295) found no significant effect of frequency in volume-equated studies using direct hypertrophy measures (ES = 0.07 [95% CI −0.08, 0.21]). Conclusion: for a given volume, weekly frequency per muscle can be chosen by preference. In non-volume-equated studies higher frequencies were favoured, but the gap between 1 and 3+ days/week was modest. **[Strong]** — [PubMed 30558493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/How-many-times-per-week-should-a-muscle-be-trained-Schoenfeld-Grgic/5f3b9845fdfd15200bdac0f7b9b36da457c48025)
- Earlier Schoenfeld 2016 frequency meta-analysis (Sports Med) compared 1x vs 2x/week per muscle and favoured 2x/week. This is the origin of the common "train each muscle at least twice weekly" heuristic. The 2019 update showed the advantage was largely a volume effect. **[Moderate]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effects-of-Resistance-Training-Frequency-on-of-A-Schoenfeld-Ogborn/384a4adc7317f92e5b56533b54367e6eedeff73a). (The exact 2016 effect sizes were not retrieved.)
- **Volume dose-response (hypertrophy)**: Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 analysed 15 studies (34 treatment groups). Each extra weekly set gave ES +0.023 (≈ +0.37% muscle size, p = 0.002). Low volumes (≤4 weekly sets per muscle) still produced substantial gains, with a graded dose-response above that. Data were sparse above ~10–12 weekly sets. **[Moderate]** — [Scinergy summary](https://www.scinergy.io/learn/sets-per-week-for-muscle-gain); [CORE PDF](https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/200371264.pdf)
- **Pelland et al. (2024 preprint; published in Sports Medicine 2025, doi 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w)**: meta-regression of 67 studies, 2,058 participants.
  - Volume: 100% posterior probability that both hypertrophy and strength rise with volume. Both show diminishing returns, much steeper for strength than for size.
  - Frequency: a negligible relationship with hypertrophy, but a 100% posterior probability that strength rises with frequency (also with diminishing returns).
  - Sets were counted "fractionally": a direct set = 1, an indirect set (e.g. triceps during bench press) = 0.5.

  **[Moderate–Strong]** — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460); [PubMed 41343037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)
- Secondary summaries of Pelland report three more numbers:
  - Strength: no detectable superiority beyond ~3 fractional weekly sets (very steep diminishing returns).
  - Frequency: each extra training day added ~3.27% strength gain, versus only ~0.32% hypertrophy per extra weekly session (a credible interval that crosses zero).
  - Hypertrophy: the first ~10 fractional weekly sets per muscle bring the largest return.

  **[Moderate; numbers from secondary sources, not verified against the full text]** — [FitChef summary](https://fitchef.com/studies/training-volume-sets-per-week-study/); [Stronger by Science volume guide](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/volume/)
- **Per-session volume (Pelland/Remmert companion paper)**: the "point of unique optimality" is about 2 sets per muscle per session for strength and about 11 sets per muscle per session for hypertrophy. Above this, extra sets in the same session add progressively less. **[Moderate–Weak; preprint]** — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)
- **Split vs full body**: Ramos-Campo et al. 2024 (JSCR; 14 studies, 392 subjects) found split and full-body routines give similar strength and hypertrophy when volume is equated. Volume matters more than routine type. **[Moderate–Strong]** — [JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2024/07000/efficacy_of_split_versus_full_body_resistance.20.aspx); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379724552_Efficacy_of_Split_Versus_Full-Body_Resistance_Training_on_Strength_and_Muscle_Growth_A_Systematic_Review_With_Meta-Analysis)
- **ACSM 2026 Position Stand**: "Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews", MSSE, 17 March 2026. It draws on 137 systematic reviews and 30,000+ participants, and is the first update since 2009.
  - Train all major muscle groups at least twice a week.
  - Hypertrophy: higher weekly volume, ~10 sets per muscle group.
  - Strength: heavier loads (~80% 1RM) for 2–3 sets per exercise.
  - Training to failure, machines vs free weights, and periodization "did not consistently impact outcomes for the average healthy adult".
  - Key message: "The best resistance training program is the one you'll actually stick with."

  **[Strong — overview of reviews]** — [ACSM press release](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/); [Ovid/MSSE](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/acsm-msse/fulltext/10.1249/mss.0000000000003897~american-college-of-sports-medicine-position-stand)
  - Caution: some secondary blogs attribute tiered ranges to ACSM 2026 (beginners 10–15, intermediate 15–20, advanced 15–25+ sets per muscle per week) and "2–3 sessions per muscle per week as optimal". These ranges do **not** appear on the ACSM press release page I fetched, so treat them as unverified. — [getfitcraft (aggregator)](https://getfitcraft.com/science/acsm-2026-resistance-training-guidelines)
- **Proximity to failure**:
  - Strength gains are similar across a wide range of RIR.
  - Hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure. Refalo et al. 2023 (Sports Med) found a small hypertrophy edge for sets closer to failure (ES ~0.15–0.21).
  - The Robinson et al. 2023 meta-regression had negative RIR slopes for hypertrophy, but RIR estimation in the studies is uncertain.

  **[Moderate]** — [Robinson et al. preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295); [Refalo 2024 RCT, J Sports Sci](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021)

### Inferences
- For template design, ASCEND should guarantee **≥2 exposures per major muscle group per week** in the Kracht program. This hits the ACSM floor and captures the frequency benefit for strength, and it keeps per-session volume moderate, which matters more given the per-session diminishing returns.
- Since MacroFactor sets the exercise volume, ASCEND only needs a coarse check: Kracht ≈ 10–20 weekly hard sets per major muscle; Fit en gezond ≈ 4–10.
- Split is a free choice once the weekly exposure count is met, so ASCEND can pick the split from the number of days available rather than by "goal".
- Frequency helps strength more than size. For a strength-leaning user, prefer more frequent exposures to the main lifts (e.g. squat/hinge/press 2x/week) over one big weekly session.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full published Pelland 2025 text to confirm the exact marginal-gain numbers (3.27%, 0.32%, ~10 sets). They come from secondary summaries.
- No good evidence on optimal volume for *recreational* lifters specifically, versus trained young men. Most trials use young, mostly male participants.

## 2. Recommended split by days available (3 / 4 / 5 days)

### Takeaway
All splits work when weekly volume and ≥2x/week frequency per muscle are met (moderate–strong evidence). The choice by days available is expert opinion grounded in that constraint:
- 3 days: full body ×3, or Upper / Lower / Full
- 4 days: Upper/Lower ×2
- 5 days: Upper / Lower / Push / Pull / Legs (ULPPL), or Upper/Lower ×2 + 1 full-body/weak-point day

### Cited Findings
- Split vs full body is equivalent when volume is equated. — [Ramos-Campo 2024, JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2024/07000/efficacy_of_split_versus_full_body_resistance.20.aspx)
- Frequency can be chosen by preference for a given volume. — [Schoenfeld 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)
- Train each major muscle at least 2x/week. — [ACSM 2026](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)
- Per-session diminishing returns: spreading weekly sets over more sessions is more efficient than concentrating them. — [Pelland/Remmert preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)

### Inferences (expert opinion — concrete templates)

ASCEND only schedules the session *type*. MacroFactor fills in exercises. Leg-heavy sessions (Lower, Legs, Full body with squat/hinge, long hikes) must respect ASCEND's existing 48-hour leg rule.

**Kracht — 3 days (full body ×3).** Each muscle gets 3 exposures/week, so it is the most time-efficient option.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full Body A (squat focus) | Easy cardio Z2 30–45' (cycling) | Full Body B (hinge focus) | Rest / walk | Full Body C (squat/press variation) | Long easy cardio or hike 60–90' | Rest |

- Leg-heavy every 48 h (Mon–Wed–Fri) is at the edge of the 48-hour rule. Sat must then be *non-leg-heavy* if Fri counts as leg-heavy. Practical option: make Full Body B "hinge + upper-dominant, light legs", or move the weekend hike to Sun with Fri → Sun = 48 h.
- Alternative 3-day layout: **Upper / Lower / Full** (Mon Upper, Wed Lower, Fri Full). Each muscle gets 2 exposures and there are only 2 leg-heavy days, which leaves more room for a hike.

**Kracht — 4 days (Upper/Lower ×2).** Each muscle gets 2 exposures, about 5–8 sets per muscle per session.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper A | Lower A | Rest or Z2 cycling 30' | Upper B | Lower B | Long Z2 / hike (light) or rest | Rest |

- Lower A (Tue) to Lower B (Fri) = 72 h, which is fine.
- A hike on Sat would be <48 h after Lower B (Fri). Either swap to **Mon Lower A / Tue Upper A / Thu Lower B / Fri Upper B** with the hike on Sat (Thu→Sat = 48 h), or keep Sat as non-leg cardio (e.g. easy cycling, swimming).
- Recommended default for ASCEND: **Mon Lower A, Tue Upper A, Thu Lower B, Fri Upper B, Sat hike / long easy cardio, Wed + Sun optional Z2 or rest.**

**Kracht — 5 days (ULPPL: Upper / Lower / Push / Pull / Legs).** Each muscle gets ~2 exposures with more total volume.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper | Lower | Rest or Z2 | Push | Pull | Legs | Rest |

- Lower (Tue) → Legs (Sat) is fine. A Sunday hike would conflict with Sat Legs (<48 h), so a hike week needs Legs moved (e.g. Thu Legs, Fri Push, Sat Pull) or the hike treated as the leg session.
- Alternative 5-day layout: U/L/U/L + Full Body/arms-shoulders "pump" day (non-leg-heavy).

**Fit en gezond — 2 days (full body ×2).**

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full Body A | Brisk walk/cycle 30' | Rest | Full Body B | Brisk walk/cycle 30' | Longer walk/hike/cycle 45–90' | Rest |

**Fit en gezond — 3 days**: Full Body A / B / A (alternating), plus 2–3 cardio bouts.

### Gaps
- No trials compare ULPPL vs U/L ×2 vs PPL directly in recreational adults. The 5-day choice is coaching convention.
- The interaction between ASCEND's 48-hour leg rule and full-body ×3 is not studied. Treating every full-body session as "leg-heavy" may be too strict. Consider a "leg-moderate" flag.

## 3. Cardio for strength-focused trainees: how much, which modality, when

### Takeaway
- **How much**: keep WHO-level aerobic activity for health, i.e. 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous per week, or a mix. Concurrent training does not meaningfully blunt hypertrophy or maximal strength in recent meta-analyses (moderate–strong). Explosive strength may suffer when both are done in the same session.
- **Modality**: interference is smallest with cycling and other low-impact modes, and largest with high-volume running (moderate, older data).
- **Timing**: separate cardio and lifting by ≥6 h, ideally 24 h for hard cardio. Keep hard or long cardio away from the 24–48 h before a leg day (moderate/weak).

### Cited Findings
- **WHO 2020**: adults should do 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 min/week vigorous aerobic activity (or a combination), plus muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity on ≥2 days/week. Some activity is better than none and more is better. **[Strong — guideline]** — [WHO 2020 guidelines, BJSM/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/); [WHO IRIS](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/faa83413-d89e-4be9-bb01-b24671aef7ca/content)
- **Schumann et al. 2022 (Sports Med; 43 studies)**:
  - Concurrent aerobic + strength training did not compromise hypertrophy or maximal strength development.
  - Explosive strength gains may be attenuated, especially when both are done in the same session.
  - No interference for untrained subjects, and no interference for trained subjects when sessions were split.

  **[Moderate–Strong]** — [Stronger by Science research spotlight](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-interference-effect/); [Schumann PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Effects-of-concurrent-training-on-maximal-and.pdf)
- **Lundberg et al. 2022 fibre-level meta-analysis**: concurrent training may blunt type I fibre hypertrophy, and this "may be more pronounced when aerobic training is performed by running compared with cycling" (preliminary). **[Weak–Moderate]** — [PubMed 35476184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35476184/)
- **Wilson et al. 2012 (JSCR; 21 studies, 422 ES)**:
  - Concurrent training *with running*, but not cycling, significantly reduced hypertrophy and strength.
  - Endurance frequency (r ≈ −0.26 to −0.35) and duration (r ≈ −0.29 to −0.75) were negatively related to hypertrophy, strength and power.
  - Hypertrophy ES: strength-only 1.23, concurrent 0.85, endurance-only 0.27.

  **[Moderate; older, partly superseded by Schumann 2022, which found little interference overall]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51719597_Concurrent_Training_A_Meta-Analysis_Examining_Interference_of_Aerobic_and_Resistance_Exercises)
- **Timing**: Robineau et al. 2016 compared 0, 6 and 24 h between strength and endurance work. Strength/power adaptations were inhibited unless ≥6 h separated sessions. 6 h was enough for strength outcomes, and 24 h gave the best VO₂max improvement. Recommendation: avoid scheduling two "contradictory qualities" <6 h apart. **[Weak–Moderate; single RCT]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Specific-Training-Effects-of-Concurrent-Aerobic-and-Robineau-Babault/745aa1a9c8ec56a8815895fe71b4cd5feeea9722); [FFR PDF](https://formation.ffr.fr/sites/default/files/documents/doc/2016-12/Specific%20training%20effects%20of%20concurrent%20aerobic%20and%20strength%20exercises%20depend%20on%20recovery%20duration.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Kracht cardio dose**: aim at the WHO minimum, ~150 min moderate per week (e.g. 2–3 × 30–45 min zone-2 cycling/walking/rowing + 1 longer walk or hike), or ~75 min vigorous (e.g. 1–2 short interval sessions). Going above ~300 min moderate per week is unnecessary for health and adds interference risk through more frequency and duration (Wilson's correlations).
- **Modality ranking for the Kracht template**, least to most interference: walking / easy cycling / rowing / swimming → incline walking / hiking → running (especially long or downhill runs, because of eccentric damage). Hikes with big descents are eccentric-heavy and should count as leg-heavy under the existing scheduler rule.
- **Timing rules for ASCEND (expert opinion based on Robineau + Schumann)**:
  - Put cardio on non-lifting days or ≥6 h apart from lifting.
  - If done the same day, do it *after* lifting, and easy.
  - No hard or long leg cardio (intervals, long runs, steep hikes) in the 24 h before a Lower or Legs session.
  - Easy Z2 cycling the day after a leg day is fine and may aid recovery.
- One vigorous interval session a week (e.g. 4–6 × 3–4 min hard on the bike) covers the "vigorous" component efficiently. Schedule it after an upper day or on a rest day, not before a leg day.

### Gaps
- No meta-analytic dose threshold for cardio min/week above which hypertrophy is impaired in recreational lifters.
- Modality evidence (running vs cycling) rests on older/preliminary analyses. Schumann 2022's modality subgroup results were not verifiable in full text.

## 4. Deloads, periodization, and how often to change the program

### Takeaway
Deloads are mainly expert opinion. The consensus is every 4–6 weeks, ~7 days, with volume cut and intensity possibly kept. The only RCT (Coleman 2024) found a 1-week complete break had no effect on hypertrophy but slightly reduced strength, so a "light training" deload beats full rest. For periodization: linear and undulating give equal hypertrophy at equal volume. Undulating gives a small strength edge in trained lifters only, and ACSM 2026 says periodization does not consistently matter for the average healthy adult. A 4-week phase with planned or flexible light weeks fits all of this.

### Cited Findings
- **Bell et al. 2023 Delphi (Sports Med Open; 34 → 29 → 21 expert coaches over 3 rounds)**:
  - Definition: "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training."
  - Timing: generally every 4–6 weeks for ~7 days (range from single sessions to 2 weeks).
  - Volume: 100% agreement that volume should drop, via fewer sets, fewer reps or fewer training days; 90.5% agreed on a "minimum effective dose".
  - Intensity: 81% agreed intensity can stay high.
  - Planning: 100% agreed deloads can be pre-planned *and* autoregulatory, and 100% agreed they can be taken "when athletes feel physically and mentally fatigued regardless of training week".

  **[Expert opinion]** — [PMC10511399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0)
- **Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ; n = 39, 9-week high-volume program)**: a 1-week *complete* training cessation at the midpoint gave:
  - no appreciable difference in lower-body hypertrophy, local endurance or power;
  - greater isometric and dynamic lower-body strength gains in the continuous-training group;
  - no evidence of "re-sensitization" benefit.

  **[Weak–Moderate; single RCT, untrained/recreational young adults]** — [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/16777/)
- A 2026 within-subject RCT in untrained young men (Scientific Reports) also examined deload periods and hypertrophy/strength-endurance. Details were not extracted. — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40612-5); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13031491/)
- **Periodization (Moesgaard et al. 2022, Sports Med 52:1647–1666)**, volume-equated designs:
  - Undulating periodization gave greater strength gains than linear only in resistance-trained individuals, not in untrained.
  - Periodization did not affect hypertrophy.

  **[Moderate]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357932732_Effects_of_Periodization_on_Strength_and_Muscle_Hypertrophy_in_Volume-Equated_Resistance_Training_Programs_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-analysis); [RegionH](https://research.regionh.dk/en/publications/effects-of-periodization-on-strength-and-muscle-hypertrophy-in-vo/)
- **ACSM 2026**: periodization "did not consistently impact outcomes for the average healthy adult". Consistency and effort drive results. **[Strong]** — [ACSM](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

### Inferences (concrete 4-week phase model for ASCEND — expert opinion)
- **Kracht 4-week phase**:
  - Week 1 "Opbouw" (base volume, ~2–3 RIR).
  - Week 2 "Opbouw+" (MacroFactor progresses load/reps; ASCEND keeps the same session count).
  - Week 3 "Piek" (optionally +1 set on key lifts or 1 extra session, closest to failure, ~1–2 RIR).
  - Week 4 "Ontlast/Deload" (same days but ~40–50% fewer sets, same or slightly lower loads, 3–4 RIR; cardio stays easy).
  - Coleman 2024 suggests keeping training during deloads rather than taking full rest when strength is a priority.
- **Alternative**: a **3:1 or 5:1 flexible deload**. Plan a deload every 4th week by default, but allow the user to skip it if readiness is high, or pull it forward if fatigue signals appear (Bell: "regardless of training week"). For recreational lifters with moderate volume, a deload every 6–8 weeks or only on demand is defensible. The evidence that frequent deloads are needed at all is weak.
- **Program changes**: keep the same template (exercise selection) for at least one or two 4-week phases (4–12 weeks) so Smart Progression can track trends. Rotate session variations (e.g. A/B variants, rep-range emphasis) between phases rather than within them. Phase-to-phase variation in rep ranges (e.g. phase 1 hypertrophy 8–12 reps, phase 2 strength 4–8 reps) is a lightweight "block/undulating" approach consistent with Moesgaard.
- **Fit en gezond**: no structured deload needed. Use an "easy week" when life gets busy, sick, or after travel. One easy week per 6–8 weeks is optional.

### Gaps
- No RCT evidence on "light-training" deloads (reduced volume) versus continuous training for strength. The Coleman study only tested full cessation.
- No evidence on the optimal mesocycle length (4 vs 6 vs 8 weeks) for recreational lifters. The 4-week phase is convention.

## 5. Progress signals for strength-focused training and when to reduce

### Takeaway
The best signal is performance trend: reps/load at a given RIR on key lifts, as tracked by MacroFactor Smart Progression. Combine it with simple subjective markers (sleep, soreness, motivation/readiness). Reduce load or volume, or trigger a deload, when performance stalls or regresses for ~2 consecutive weeks together with poor subjective readiness. Evidence for specific thresholds is expert opinion.

### Cited Findings
- Hypertrophy improves as sets approach failure, while strength gains are similar across a wide range of RIR. Training ~0–3 RIR is effective, so RIR is a useful dial for effort. — [Robinson et al. 2023 preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295); [Refalo 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021)
- RIR predictions are imperfect. The literature highlights uncertainty in RIR estimation, which argues for performance trends over single-session RIR values. — [Refalo RIR accuracy study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Martin-Refalo-2/publication/375687905_Accuracy_of_Intraset_Repetitions-in-Reserve_Predictions_During_the_Bench_Press_Exercise_in_Resistance-Trained_Male_and_Female_Subjects/links/65d9591fadc608480ae7db13/Accuracy-of-Intraset-Repetitions-in-Reserve-Predictions-During-the-Bench-Press-Exercise-in-Resistance-Trained-Male-and-Female-Subjects.pdf)
- Experts agree a deload can be triggered when athletes "feel physically and mentally fatigued regardless of training week" (100% consensus). Deloads can be autoregulated. — [Bell 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/)
- In Coleman 2024, the readiness-to-train questionnaire showed only slight differences between groups. The deload did not improve psychological readiness. — [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/16777/)

### Inferences (signal → action rules for ASCEND; expert opinion)
- **Green** (continue): key-lift performance stable or rising, sleep ≥7 h most nights, soreness gone before the next session for that muscle.
- **Amber** (hold, do not add volume or extra sessions): a single bad session, poor sleep for 2–3 nights, or soreness still present at the next same-muscle session. Keep the plan but skip optional cardio or extra sets.
- **Red** (deload or reduce): performance down on ≥2 key lifts across ≥2 consecutive sessions or weeks, *plus* persistent fatigue, low motivation or poor sleep. Pull the deload week forward, or cut weekly sets ~30–50% for a week.
- ASCEND's readiness engine (strength/consistency/recovery over 28 days) can use **consistency** (sessions logged vs planned) as the main Kracht metric. Load progression lives in MacroFactor, so ASCEND should not duplicate it.

### Gaps
- No validated thresholds (e.g. "% drop in e1RM") for recreational lifters for when to deload.
- No research linking wearable HRV / sleep data to deload timing in strength training. Any such rule would be expert opinion.

## 6. General fitness / health variant ("Fit en gezond"): minimum effective dose and progression

### Takeaway
For health, 2 full-body strength sessions per week plus 150 min moderate cardio per week is the guideline floor (strong). Strength improves even with 1 set per exercise, 2–3x/week, or ~4 weekly sets per muscle (moderate). Progress first by consistency, then by adding sets (1 → 2–3 per exercise), then a third session. A 1x/week fallback still produces strength gains (weak–moderate).

### Cited Findings
- WHO 2020: muscle-strengthening at moderate+ intensity on ≥2 days/week, plus 150–300 min moderate / 75–150 min vigorous aerobic. — [WHO 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/)
- ACSM 2026: all major muscle groups at least twice a week. Consistency matters more than program complexity. — [ACSM](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)
- Iversen, Norum & Schoenfeld 2021 (Sports Med, "No Time to Lift?") identified a single set of 6–12 reps at 70–85% 1RM, 2–3x/week, as the minimum effective dose to raise 1RM. That figure was for resistance-trained men; untrained people respond to even less. The review recommends multi-joint exercises and time-efficient methods (supersets, short sessions). **[Moderate — narrative review]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352391197_No_Time_to_Lift_Designing_Time-Efficient_Training_Programs_for_Strength_and_Hypertrophy_A_Narrative_Review)
- Low volumes (≤4 weekly sets per muscle) produce substantial hypertrophy, with more volume giving more growth. — [Schoenfeld 2017 summary](https://www.scinergy.io/learn/sets-per-week-for-muscle-gain)
- A minimal-dose overview (Sports Med 2024) lists strategies that all raise strength in the general population:
  - "Weekend warrior": 1 day/week, 1–3 sets, 4+ exercises at 60–80%.
  - Single-set: 2–3 days/week, 1 set × 7–10 exercises at 60–85%.
  - Exercise "snacks": 5–7 days/week, 2–10 min.

  Example trials: 1 set × 13 exercises 3x/week in older adults gave 17–18% strength gains over 24 weeks, and 8 exercises × 3 sets once a week gave 21–74% gains. Conclusion: "something is better than nothing". **[Moderate; mostly older adults]** — [PMC11127831](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11127831/)

### Inferences (concrete Fit en gezond program — expert opinion grounded in the above)
- **Default**: 2 × full body (45 min) on non-consecutive days, 5–8 exercises, 1–3 sets each, ~2–3 RIR, plus 3 × 30–50 min moderate cardio (walk, cycle, swim) or a weekend hike, for ≥150 min per week in total.
- **Example week**: Mon Full Body A, Tue walk/cycle 40', Thu Full Body B, Fri walk 30', Sat longer hike/cycle 60–90', Wed/Sun rest.
- **Progression across 4-week phases**:
  - Phase 1: 2× full body, 1–2 sets per exercise; cardio 3 × 30'.
  - Phase 2: 2–3 sets per exercise; cardio 3 × 40', or add 1 short vigorous bout.
  - Phase 3: optional 3rd full-body day, or move up to the Kracht 3-day template; cardio to ~200 min per week or 1 interval session.
- **Fallback when busy**: 1 full-body session (≈3 sets per main exercise) plus walks maintains and still builds strength. ASCEND's scheduler could offer this as a "minimum week".
- No deloads needed. Use "easy weeks" only when sick or travelling.

### Gaps
- Most minimal-dose evidence comes from older or untrained populations. Its applicability to fit recreational adults is assumed.
- Iversen's single-set figure applies to trained men. I could not open the full text to extract set recommendations per muscle group for hypertrophy.
