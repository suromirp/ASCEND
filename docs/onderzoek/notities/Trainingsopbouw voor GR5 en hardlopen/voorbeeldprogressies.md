# Example progressions for phases 2-4 (Opbouw, Bergcapaciteit, Expeditieklaar): GR5 trek + running

Context used for the inferences below: a recreational adult who has finished phase 1 (weeks 1-4). The target event day is about 20 km, about 1,000 m D+ and D-, with a pack of about 12 kg. Each phase is a 4-week wave: easy, build, hardest, deload. Evidence level is marked as **[expert/practice]** (coaches, guides, books), **[research]** (peer-reviewed), or **[aggregator/blog]** (lower confidence).

## 1. Uphill Athlete / House & Johnston: base structure, AeT, Muscular Endurance (ME), pack progression, vertical

### Takeaway
Uphill Athlete (UA) uses this structure: 4 aerobic days (Zone 1-2, mostly below aerobic threshold), 2 strength days and 1 rest day per week. The longest session is about 50% of weekly time, and every 3rd or 4th week volume drops by about 50%. The pack starts at 0% bodyweight (BW) and goes to 5%, then 10%, then 15-20% BW in 2-week steps, capped at about 20-25% BW or the trek load. ME is a separate 6-8 week block of 1 session per week, done late in the plan (it starts in week 16 of their 24-week plan). The ME session is steep uphill with weight, starting at 5-10% BW, and only one variable (weight or vertical) is raised at a time. All of this is [expert/practice].

### Cited Findings
- **Weekly structure (trekking):** "Spread your training over six days per week: 4 days aerobic base (Zone 1–2), 2 days strength, and 1 full rest day"; "Aim for four aerobic workouts per week", with the longest session "around 50% of your total weekly training time". [expert/practice] — [Uphill Athlete: Training for Trekking and Hiking](https://uphillathlete.com/trekking/training-for-trekking-and-hiking/)
- **Volume:** start at 5–8 h/week and build to "10–12+ hours per week for more demanding treks". The plan should run 16–24 weeks at best and 8 weeks at minimum. [expert/practice] — [UA Trekking](https://uphillathlete.com/trekking/training-for-trekking-and-hiking/)
- **Recovery weeks:** "Every third or fourth week, reduce your total training time by ~50%". [expert/practice] — [UA Trekking](https://uphillathlete.com/trekking/training-for-trekking-and-hiking/). The mountaineering page describes the same pattern: 3 weeks of progression, then a recovery week at "50% of the training volume". — [UA: Training for Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)
- **Pack progression (trekking page):** weeks 1–4 bodyweight only; weeks 5–6 +5% BW; weeks 7–8 +10% BW; weeks 9–10 +15–20% BW. The maximum is "up to 20% of your body weight or up to the maximum weight you will carry on your trek". Beginners should train at about "15% less than you expect to carry". [expert/practice] — [UA Trekking](https://uphillathlete.com/trekking/training-for-trekking-and-hiking/)
- **Pack progression (mountaineering page):** 0% → 5% → 10% → 15% BW over the same 10 weeks. Long aerobic hikes are capped at about 25% BW. Light athletes (<120 lb / 54 kg) may add pack weight faster. [expert/practice] — [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)
- **AeT re-testing:** about every 8–12 weeks, with at least 6 weeks between tests for a meaningful comparison. [expert/practice] — [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)
- **ME prerequisite:** UA recommends that "your AeT be within 10 percent of your anaerobic threshold (AnT), measured by heart rate, before beginning an ME program". [expert/practice] — [UA: Muscular Endurance Training for Mountain Athletes](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/vertical-beast-mode-what-is-muscular-endurance-why-it-is-important-for-any-alpinist-or-mountaineer-and-how-do-you-train-it/)
- **Outdoor ME (hill carry with water jugs):** "Start with 5 to 10 percent of body weight if you are new to this type of training", and "many fit climbers work comfortably in the 10 to 30 percent range". The goal is a load "at or above the weight you will carry on your objective", on slopes "ranging from 30 to 100 percent grade (17 to 45 degrees)". The block is "6 to 10 workouts spanning roughly six to eight weeks", at "one ME session per week", followed by "at least three days of easy recovery workouts after each ME session". The rule is to "progress either the weight or the total vertical in a stairstep fashion, but not both at the same time". The final workouts should include "more vertical and more weight than the biggest day of your upcoming objective". Effort guide: "able to carry on a conversation while your legs are burning". Heart rate is unreliable for ME work. Water is dumped at the top to protect the legs on the descent. [expert/practice] — [UA ME article](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/vertical-beast-mode-what-is-muscular-endurance-why-it-is-important-for-any-alpinist-or-mountaineer-and-how-do-you-train-it/)
- **ME timing and dose in the 24-week mountaineering plan:** ME is introduced around week 16. The goal is to "work up to about 60min of up time" on grades steeper than "10-12%" (treadmill 15%), where the limit is "local muscular fatigue in your legs, not by your breathing". Heavy pack carries of 40–70 lb are kept separate from aerobic base work. [expert/practice] — [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)
- **Gym/at-home ME (step-up circuit):** use a box at "75 percent of the height of the bottom of your kneecap" and a tempo of "1 rep every 1–2 seconds". Sets of 10 reps progress from 4 sets (bodyweight, WO 1–2) to 5 sets (WO 3) to 5–7 sets with added load (WO 4–9) to 8 sets (WO 10). Rest drops from 60 s to 45 s to 30 s. "Once a week is enough for substantial progress." [expert/practice] — [UA At-Home ME Workout with Progression](https://uphillathlete.com/strength-training/at-home-muscular-endurance-workout-with-progression/)
  - *Conflict:* the summary I fetched of the at-home article lists added loads of +10% → +20% → +30% → +45% → +60% → +80% → +100% (WO 4–10). Those numbers likely refer to the load of a specific accessory exercise, or to % increases, not % BW. The UA ME PDF workout (search snippet) instead gives +10% BW for WO 5–6 (6–8 × 10, 40 s rest) and +20% BW for WO 9–10 (7–8 × 10). — [UA ME PDF](https://uphillathlete.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/ME-Uphill-Athlete-Workout-.pdf). The writer should use **+10% → +20% BW** for step-ups and treat the higher numbers as unverified.
- **Event-specific simulation (mountaineering):** do "at least one mountain climbing workout per week" that matches summit-day demands (their example: "4,000 vertical feet" with a "25 pounds" pack). Taper for a 2–3-day climb: 1 week at "50% of the training volume" of the heaviest week. [expert/practice] — [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)
- **Trekking taper:** "Final 1–2 weeks before your hike, reduce training volume but maintain the intensity". [expert/practice] — [UA Trekking](https://uphillathlete.com/trekking/training-for-trekking-and-hiking/)
- **Strength ceiling:** UA "never train[s] athletes to lift more than 2x body weight" in squat or deadlift. [expert/practice] — [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)

### Inferences
- The user's 16-week plan has 4-week waves with a deload in week 4. The UA rule of 3 build weeks and 1 week at about 50% maps directly onto it. Phase 1 deloads were about 65–75% of the peak week (e.g., 60 → 40 min, 70 → 45 min), which is milder than UA's 50%. Both are defensible.
- The UA pack schedule was written for weeks 1–10 of a plan where week 1 has no pack. Shifted onto this program (no pack in phase 1), a natural fit is about 5% BW in phase 2, 10% → 15% BW in phase 3, and the full 12 kg in phase 4. For an 80 kg adult, 12 kg = 15% BW, which is inside UA's 20% maximum. For a 65 kg adult, 12 kg ≈ 18.5% BW, close to the 20% cap.
- The UA ME block is 6–10 sessions, once a week, over 6–8 weeks. That fits phases 3–4 (weeks 9–15) with about 6–7 sessions, skipping deload weeks. The UA prerequisite (AeT within 10% of AnT) may not be met by a recreational adult. If it is not, a reasonable compromise is a gentler ME dose (step-ups at bodyweight to +10% BW) with more Zone 2 volume.
- The UA end goal for ME is more vertical and more weight than the biggest event day. For the GR5 that means about 12 kg on steep terrain, with enough repeated uphill to cover the event demand. Short repeats with water jugs that are dumped at the top can do this without the descent damage of a full 1,000 m day.

### Gaps
- I could not open the "Training for the New Alpinism" / "Training for the Uphill Athlete" books. Book-specific numbers, such as their weekly vertical-meter ratio or long-session percentage tables, are not verified here. The 8-week Mont Blanc plan URL returned 404.
- UA gives no specific weekly vertical-gain target (m/week) for trekking that I could find. The numbers above are per session.

## 2. Trekking guide plans (TMB, Kilimanjaro, etc.): weekly D+, longest hike vs event day, pack progression, back-to-back

### Takeaway
Guide companies converge on these points. Build the long hike to roughly one full event day (about 800–1,000 m D+ in 5–6 h, or about 900 m D+ in 3 h with an 11 kg pack). Add 1–1.5 kg of pack per hike or about 10% vertical per week. Include back-to-back days in the last 4–6 weeks: day 1 heavy with lots of vertical, day 2 longer and lighter. Then taper. All of this is [expert/practice], with no peer-reviewed support.

### Cited Findings
- **Alpine Ascents (Kilimanjaro):** start with hikes of up to 1,000 ft (≈300 m) of gain and increase "approximately 10% per week". The pack starts at 10 lb (≈4.5 kg) and adds "2-3 lbs. per hike" (≈1–1.4 kg). Reach 20 lb (≈9 kg) once hikes hit 3,000 ft (≈900 m), and 25 lb (≈11 kg) one month before the trek. Target: "ascend 3,000 feet, carrying an average pack of 25 lbs. in a three-hour period" (≈1,000 ft/h ≈ 300 m/h). Back-to-back weekends start one month before: Sat "Hike 6–8 miles, 20-lb. pack, gain 3,000 ft." (≈10–13 km, 9 kg, 900 m); Sun "Hike 8–10 miles, 15-lb. pack, gain 3,500 ft." (≈13–16 km, 7 kg, 1,050 m). Cardio goes from 20–30 min ×3/week to 45–60 min ×4–5/week. Taper in the final week. [expert/practice] — [Alpine Ascents: Kilimanjaro Training](https://www.alpineascents.com/climbs/mount-kilimanjaro/training/)
- **Tour du Mont Blanc (commercial guide site):** in the 2–3 months before the trek, the long hike builds to "5–6 hours with 800–1,000 m of elevation gain". The TMB itself totals about 10,000 m D+. Plan for 8–12 weeks of walking 4–5 days per week, and 3–4 sessions per week with deliberate elevation gain. [expert/practice; commercial; from search summary] — [Tours du Mont Blanc: Training Plan](https://toursdumontblanc.com/blog/training-plan)
- **TMB (blog):** a weekly long hike that starts 3 months out and builds to an "11-mile, 4,000-foot" hike (≈18 km, ≈1,200 m) in the weeks before the trip, "equivalent to the TMB's longest day". A multi-day mountain trip in the final 4–6 weeks is recommended. "Even two days in hilly terrain with your full pack and actual boots" is valuable. [aggregator/blog; from search summary] — [Life Happens Outdoors: TMB Training Plan](https://lifehappensoutdoors.com/the-tour-du-mont-blanc-training-plan/)

### Inferences
- Applied to the GR5 day (20 km, 1,000 m, 12 kg), the guide consensus gives these phase-4 targets: one hike at about 1,000 m D+ with close to the full pack (100% of one event day's vertical), plus 1–2 back-to-back weekends at about 60–80% of event vertical per day (e.g., 750 m + 600–750 m). That matches the planned phase-4 "500 → 750 → 1,000+ m" sequence.
- Alpine Ascents' weekly +10% vertical with +1–1.4 kg per hike is a concrete rule the scheduler could encode. Starting at about 300–400 m in phase 3 week 1, +10% per week reaches only about 550 m by week 15. Reaching 1,000 m therefore needs the larger jumps the user already planned (500 → 750 → 1,000). These are +50% and +33% steps, well above the guide rule of thumb. It is safer to spread the D+ ramp across phases 3–4: e.g., phase 3 at 300 → 450 → 600 → 300 m, then phase 4 at 500 → 750 → 1,000 → taper.
- Speed benchmark: 300 m/h with an 11 kg pack (Alpine Ascents) means 1,000 m D+ takes about 3.3 h of climbing alone. Adding descent and flat sections gives 5–7 h for a GR5-like day, consistent with the TMB "5–6 h, 800–1,000 m" long-hike target.

### Gaps
- I found no GR5-specific training plan from a reputable source (e.g., FFRandonnée or Dutch/Belgian walking federations).
- I did not verify REI's or the British Mountaineering Council's / Alpine Club's training guidance, or EBC plans from guides such as Adventure Consultants. The figures above rely on Alpine Ascents and TMB-specific sites.

## 3. Hill intervals → threshold/tempo progression (Daniels, hill repeat progressions)

### Takeaway
Hill reps usually lengthen step by step: 30 s → 45 s → 60 s → 90 s, with total reps falling and recovery growing to 2–3× the rep length. Daniels caps threshold (T) work at about 10% of weekly running volume per session: about 20 min continuous tempo, or ≥30 min as cruise intervals with a work:rest ratio of about 5:1. This is [expert/practice]. Daniels' guidance comes from his coaching books.

### Cited Findings
- **Hill repeat progression (Outside/RUN):** reps last 30 s–2 min, and recovery (jog/walk) is "double or triple the length" of the rep. Sample progression: WO1 8–12 × 30 s (1–1.5 min recovery); WO2 10–15 × 30 s; WO3 8–12 × 45 s (1.5–2 min); WO4 6–8 × 60 s (2–3 min); WO5 4–6 × 90 s (4–5 min). [expert/practice; from search summary] — [Outside Run: Hill Repeat Progression](https://run.outsideonline.com/training/workouts/hill-repeat-progression/?scope=anon)
- **Daniels threshold rules:** a tempo run is a steady run at T-pace of about 20 min. One session should include no more than 10% of weekly mileage at T-pace. Anyone who can handle 20 min at T-pace can do at least 30 min as cruise intervals: 1-mile reps with 1 min rest or 2-mile reps with 2 min rest, a work:rest ratio of about 5:1. T runs longer than 20 min can be done at a slightly slower pace. [expert/practice] — [Coach Ray: Jack Daniels' Running Intensity](https://www.coachray.nz/2023/05/03/jack-daniels-running-intensity/)

### Inferences
- Phase 1 hill intervals were 4–5 → 6 → 8 → 4 reps of 30–90 s. A logical next step for phase 2 (Opbouw) is longer reps with the same or fewer repetitions: e.g., 6 × 90 s → 5 × 2 min → 4 × 3 min (uphill at about 10K/threshold effort, jog down), then deload at 4 × 60 s. In phase 3 the "quality" running slot could become a tempo/threshold session: 2 × 8 min → 3 × 8 min → 20 min continuous, or cruise intervals of 3–4 × 6–8 min with 1–2 min rest. Daniels' 10%-of-weekly-volume cap should be respected; at about 25 km/week of running that is about 2.5 km at T-pace. In phase 4 the running-intensity slot can shrink (one short tempo or strides) because hiking load is at its peak.
- For trekking-specific goals, a "long hill" variant (sustained 10–20 min uphill hiking or running at threshold effort) links the hill intervals to the UA ME work. This is an inference; no source above prescribes it.

### Gaps
- I did not open Daniels' Running Formula or Pfitzinger's books directly. Snippets about "2–4 min long hill repeats in the base phase" appeared in search results but could not be pinned to a verified source.

## 4. Easy run and long run progression (recreational)

### Takeaway
Daniels caps the long run at 25–30% of weekly running volume and at about 2.5 h. Pfitzinger allows up to about 33% in marathon blocks. The "10% rule" is not strongly supported: a large novice-runner cohort found higher injury risk only for increases above 30% over 2 weeks, with no clear dose-response below that ([research]).

### Cited Findings
- **Daniels:** never exceed "25–30 percent of their weekly mileage" in a long run, and a "2:30–3:00-hour time limit should be enforced". [expert/practice] — [Trail Runner: Mastering the Long Run](https://www.trailrunnermag.com/training/mastering-the-long-run/)
- **Pfitzinger vs Daniels:** Pfitzinger allows up to 33% of weekly volume in marathon-specific blocks. Daniels' long runs typically max at 29–32 km or 25% of weekly volume, whichever is less, and he says runs beyond 2.5 h give diminishing returns. [aggregator/blog] — [Zarkus: Pfitzinger vs Daniels](https://zarkus.app/en/blog/pfitzinger-vs-daniels)
- **Progression rate (research):** a prospective study of 874 novice runners with GPS watches (Nielsen et al., JOSPT 2014). Runners who increased weekly distance by more than 30% over 2 weeks had a hazard ratio of 1.59 for distance-related injuries vs <10%. "No dose-response relationship was revealed": 10–30% was not more hazardous than <10%. [research] — [Nielsen et al. 2014, JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2014.5164)

### Inferences
- The phase 1 long run went 50 → 60 → 70 → 45 min, about +15–17% per week, which is under the 30% risk threshold. For phases 2–4, a reasonable sequence is: phase 2 at 70 → 80 → 90 → 60 min; phase 3 at about 80 → 90 → 100 → 60 min, or hold at 75–90 min because hikes now carry the long aerobic stimulus; phase 4 at about 60–75 min, shorter, since back-to-back hikes dominate the weekend. Each step is about +10–15%, and the long run stays well under Daniels' 2.5 h cap.
- Phase 2 plans "2× running per week". If easy runs are 35 → 40 → 45 → 30 min each and the long run is 70 → 90 min, running time is about 2.5–3 h per week. The long run would then be about 40–50% of weekly running time, above Daniels' 25–30% guideline. This is acceptable only if the hiking sessions count as aerobic volume, as in the UA "longest session ≈50% of weekly time" framework. The report writer should flag this tension.
- The 30%-over-2-weeks threshold suggests a guardrail for the scheduler: flag any week-over-week increase in a session type's volume above about 30%. This is a design suggestion, not a validated rule.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Hal Higdon's novice/intermediate long-run increments. The cited Pfitzinger figure comes from an aggregator, not the book.

## 5. Step-up / step-down (eccentric) preparation for descents

### Takeaway
Research supports deliberate, modest downhill exposure. A single 20-min downhill walk (-28% grade, 5 km/h, +10% body mass pack) reduced muscle damage from a later 40-min downhill walk by 54–61%, and the protection lasted at least 4 weeks ([research]). Gym-based step-downs and slow-lowering exercises are widely recommended by coaches ([expert/practice], with weak specific evidence).

### Cited Findings
- **Downhill walking preconditioning:** the preconditioning groups did a 20-min downhill walk (-28% slope, 5 km/h, 10% body mass in a backpack) either 1 week or 4 weeks before a 40-min downhill walk. Damage markers after the 40-min walk were 54–61% smaller than in controls. The 20-min bout itself caused 67–69% less damage than the 40-min bout in controls, and the protection "could last for more than 4 weeks". [research] — [Effect of a prior bout of preconditioning exercise on muscle damage from downhill walking (APNM 2015, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25693898/); see also [PLOS One: Prevention of downhill walking-induced muscle damage by non-damaging downhill walking](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0173909)
- **Downhill running review:** narrative review of effects and adaptation strategies for downhill running. [research, review] — [Sports Medicine 2020: Downhill Running — What Are the Effects and How Can We Adapt?](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-020-01355-z)
- **UA ME and descent:** carrying water up and dumping it at the top is done explicitly to spare the legs on the descent. The ME article therefore treats uphill strength and descent loading as separate stimuli. [expert/practice] — [UA ME article](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/vertical-beast-mode-what-is-muscular-endurance-why-it-is-important-for-any-alpinist-or-mountaineer-and-how-do-you-train-it/)
- **Step-up box height and tempo:** the box is 75% of the height of the bottom of the kneecap, at 1 rep per 1–2 s. [expert/practice] — [UA At-Home ME](https://uphillathlete.com/strength-training/at-home-muscular-endurance-workout-with-progression/)

### Inferences
- Suggested descent progression for phases 3–4. Phase 3 week 1: a short downhill bout at moderate grade (e.g., 15–20 min of descent or eccentric step-downs from a box at bodyweight, 3 × 8–10 per leg with slow lowering). Grow it with real descents on the hikes, following the "progress weight or vertical, not both" rule: first raise D- at low pack weight, then add pack. Phase 4 hikes then naturally include 500–1,000 m D-. The research suggests even one non-damaging bout gives weeks of protection, so the first loaded descent should come early (phase 3, not phase 4).
- Treadmill-only athletes (as in phase 1, incline treadmill) get no eccentric descent stimulus. This is the main gap to close in phase 3 with stairs or real descents.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed dose-response for gym step-downs (sets, reps, tempo) aimed at trekking descents. The common coach advice of a "3–4 s lowering phase" appeared only in blog search results (e.g., [Michael Moody Fitness](https://www.michaelmoodyfitness.com/personaltrainersfitnessanddietblog/hiking-conditioning-workouts-colorado), [Her Trails](https://www.hertrails.com/blog/train-the-downhill-eccentric-strength-and-the-art-of-coming-down)), and I did not verify which page says it. Treat it as low evidence.

## 6. Peak training vs event demand; load carriage (military) progressions; back-to-back days

### Takeaway
Guide plans have the athlete reach about 100% of a single event day once: Alpine Ascents uses about 3,000 ft (~900 m) with the full pack, and TMB plans use about 1,000–1,200 m. They then simulate 2 consecutive days at about 70–100% of event vertical, with the second day lighter, in the final 4–6 weeks. Military load-carriage research supports a dedicated loaded session every 7–14 days, progressing load and distance separately, combined with resistance plus aerobic training at least 3×/week for at least 4 weeks ([research], mostly military populations).

### Cited Findings
- **Load carriage review (Coyle, Pope, Orr 2011, JMVH):** loaded marching "at least two times a month" (Knapik et al. 1993, 2004). Weekly sessions improved more than fortnightly ones in some studies (Visser et al. 1995). 10-day cycles were used in the Netherlands (van Dijk 2009). Distance should "gradually increase (yet not at the same time as increase in load)". Heavier loads at lower volume gave larger gains than lighter loads over longer distances (Visser 1995). Recovery periods should be spaced through the program. Studied loads were 16–34 kg. The review covered 7 original papers from 1989–2008. [research; military] — [JMVH: Load carriage — minimising soldier injuries through physical conditioning](https://jmvh.org/article/load-carriage-minimising-soldier-injuries-through-physical-conditioning-a-narrative-review-2/)
- **Frequency evidence (Knapik):** 20 km march times improved with twice-monthly vs once-monthly loaded marching, but there was no difference between twice and four times per month. A load-carriage-specific session every 7–14 (or 10–14) days is recommended. The largest training effects came from progressive resistance training combined with aerobic training ≥3×/week over ≥4 weeks. [research; from search summary of the JMVH review and a systematic review] — [JMVH review](https://jmvh.org/article/load-carriage-minimising-soldier-injuries-through-physical-conditioning-a-narrative-review-2/); [Knapik et al., Systematic Review of the Effects of Physical Training on Load Carriage Performance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51842989_A_Systematic_Review_of_the_Effects_of_Physical_Training_on_Load_Carriage_Performance)
- **Practical ruck progression (military fitness coach):** add about 5 lb (≈2.3 kg) per week. In a sample SOF-prep plan the load goes from 20 lb (week 5) to 35 lb (week 14), never more than 5 lb per step, and load and distance never rise in the same week. [expert/practice; aggregator] — [Stew Smith: Rucking Progression](https://www.stewsmithfitness.com/blogs/news/rucking-progression-rules-of-rucking); [Whole Health Practice summary](https://www.thewholehealthpractice.com/post/rucking-once-a-week-is-enough-for-beginners-what-we-can-learn-from-training-for-special-forces-sele)
- **Back-to-back days (guide practice):** they start one month before the trip. Day 1 is 2,000–3,000 ft (≈600–900 m) with the full pack, and day 2 is longer with a lighter pack. [expert/practice] — [Alpine Ascents: Kilimanjaro](https://www.alpineascents.com/climbs/mount-kilimanjaro/training/). TMB guidance: a multi-day trip in the final 4–6 weeks, at least 2 days with full pack and boots. — [Life Happens Outdoors](https://lifehappensoutdoors.com/the-tour-du-mont-blanc-training-plan/)
- **UA:** the final ME workouts exceed the biggest day of the objective in both vertical and weight. Once a week, a simulation workout matches summit-day demands. — [UA ME](https://uphillathlete.com/aerobic-training/vertical-beast-mode-what-is-muscular-endurance-why-it-is-important-for-any-alpinist-or-mountaineer-and-how-do-you-train-it/); [UA Mountaineering](https://uphillathlete.com/mountaineering/training-for-mountaineering/)

### Inferences
- **Pack load rule for the scheduler:** two sources agree on the step size. Alpine Ascents adds 1–1.4 kg per hike and the military coach adds 2.3 kg per week, both raising load and distance/vertical separately. For a 12 kg target from about 4–5 kg (≈5% BW at 80 kg), that is about 7 kg to add. At +1–2 kg per loaded session, done once a week and skipping deload weeks, that takes about 5–7 loaded sessions, which fits phases 3–4.
- **Peak vs event:** the planned phase-4 week 3 hike (1,000+ m D+) at about 12 kg is roughly 100% of one GR5 day's vertical. A back-to-back weekend at about 750 + 600 m is about 60–75% per day and two consecutive days. This matches guide practice and the UA "match or exceed the biggest day" rule. No source supports simulating more than 2–3 consecutive days in training for a recreational trekker. The first trek days themselves serve as progressive adaptation.

| Long hike (D+) | 2 / 2.5 / 3 h, 200–400 m D+, pack ≈5% BW | 2.5 / 3 / 4 h, 300 / 450 / 600 / 300 m D+, pack 8 → 10 → 12 kg (+1–2 kg/step, not with a D+ jump) | 500 / 750 / 1,000+ m D+, 12 kg; back-to-back in wk 14–15 (day 2 lighter/longer) |
| Descent / eccentric | Step-downs BW | First 15–20 min of real descent or stairs early; step-downs | Full D- on hikes (500–1,000 m) |
| Deload | ~50–70% of week 3 | ~50% | Week 16: ~50% volume, keep some intensity |

### Gaps
- Military load-carriage studies use soldiers (mostly young men) and loads of 16–34 kg. Transfer to recreational trekkers with 12 kg is an extrapolation.
- I found no peer-reviewed study defining the "right" peak-week percentage of event demand for multi-day trekking. The 70–100% figures are guide/coach practice.
- REI, Alpine Club, and military evidence specific to back-to-back simulation (multi-day load marches) were not found in the time available.
